# Benefits
Bees make us think straight away of honey and pollen. But bees use another substance too: propolis, a sticky resin from trees.

Honeybees collect and process propolis, then use it to seal their hives. As it has antimicrobial properties, it protects the hive from the growth of fungi and bacteria. The name comes from the Greek "pro" and "polis", meaning "in front of" and "the city": propolis defends the community from any threat from outside.

Propolis has a history of more than 5,000 years. The Greeks and the Egyptians were the first to use it, as an antiseptic and to help wounds heal. The Egyptians used it to embalm their mummies and protect them from microbes and other parasites. The Greeks used it for its particularly powerful effects on the human immune system: for example, it stimulates the production of antibodies.

Our propolis is harvested in places free of contaminants. To guarantee its purity, we use patented collection trays to harvest it.

Propolis is made of nearly 150 active compounds that help the body work better, including:

- **vitamins**: provitamin A, also called the "anti-ageing" vitamin, and B vitamins, including B5, which supports the growth and resilience of the skin
- many **natural minerals** in the form of trace elements: cobalt, copper, silver, manganese, molybdenum, iron, magnesium, zinc…
- **phenolic acids** such as caffeic, cinnamic and ferulic acid
- large amounts of **flavonoids**: chrysin, pectolinarigenin, galangin… Plants produce these flavonoids as natural antiseptics that protect them from the sun's harmful rays and give them a natural defence against bacteria, moulds and fungi. In humans, they are powerful antioxidants.
