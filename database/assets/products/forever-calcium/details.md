# Benefits
Bones grow until the end of adolescence, when they reach their maximum strength. Nearly 99% of the body's calcium is in the bones and teeth; the rest is in the cells, blood, tissues and body fluids. Calcium makes up nearly 2% of the weight of the human body. Once growth ends, bones slowly lose their strength, and this can happen faster from the age of 50.

**Calcium** is a mineral whose main role, together with vitamin D, is to build the skeleton and teeth. It also contributes to the normal function of the nerves, muscles and heart. The body does not make it, so it must come from food. Foods particularly rich in calcium are dairy products, oilseeds, green vegetables and many fruits. Calcium is not fully absorbed in the intestine: only 30 to 40% passes into the blood. Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium better.

**Magnesium** is a mineral essential to the body. Nearly half of the body's magnesium is in the bones and teeth; the rest is in the muscles, liver and other soft tissues. Foods containing magnesium include cereals, pulses, seeds, nuts, dark green leafy vegetables and brewer's yeast. Magnesium contributes to the maintenance of normal bones and teeth and to normal muscle function.

**Vitamin D** is a fat-soluble vitamin that plays a key role in the body's use of calcium. The human body can make it in the skin: under the sun's UV rays, it produces vitamin D3. Foods such as dairy products and fish also contain it. Vitamin D contributes to the normal absorption and use of calcium and phosphorus, to the maintenance of normal bones and muscle function, and to normal blood calcium levels.

**Manganese** is a trace element essential to humans. The body does not make it, so it must come from food in sufficient amounts. Manganese contributes to the normal formation of connective tissue and to the maintenance of normal bones.

**Copper** is a trace element the body needs. It is found in the liver, muscles and bones, and contributes to the maintenance of normal connective tissue.

**Zinc** is an essential trace element found mostly in the muscles and bones, and also in the cells, skin, brain and pancreas. Foods rich in zinc include meat, pulses and above all whole grains. Zinc contributes to normal protein synthesis and to the maintenance of normal bones.

**Vitamin C** is a water-soluble vitamin well known for its many roles in hundreds of processes in the body. The body cannot make it, so it must come from food: colourful fruit and vegetables such as oranges, lemons, red peppers and raspberries contain the most. Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of bones and cartilage.

# How to use
Take 4 tablets a day.

**Duration:** 1 month.

**Precautions**

Do not exceed the recommended daily dose. Food supplements should be used as part of a healthy lifestyle, not as a substitute for a varied and balanced diet. Keep out of reach of young children. Magnesium supplements in less easily absorbed forms can have a laxative effect in some people. Prolonged use is not recommended. Pregnant and breastfeeding women should seek advice from a healthcare professional before taking any supplement. Not recommended for children and teenagers.
